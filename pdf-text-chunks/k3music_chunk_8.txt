GRADE 2 MUSIC (2MUSA) COURSE OVERVIEW
Course Description: 2MUSA
Music is a language that we must explore and experience to fully understand its unique characteristics. This PEI elemen-
tary music curricula embraces the discovery of the musical language using a sound before symbol, learner-centred 
process in which learners and music specialists engage in purposeful musical play.  The learners will experience and dem-
onstrate the grade level elements, concepts and skills using voice, body and instruments before identifying the musical 
language symbol. Formative elements, concepts, and skills will continue to be expanded upon throughout the elemen-
tary years. Foundational learnings need to be experienced and understood before moving onto more complex musical 
elements. 
The outcomes of the elementary music curriculum are categorized into the following 6 GCOs:
• Creating & Composing
• Musical Elements
• Musical Artistry
• Cultural Contexts & Connections
• Sharing & Performing
• Reflecting, Responding, & Analyzing
Grade two music learners will explore the musical elements by moving, using their various voices, dramatizing, visual-
izing, listening, improvising, composing, and playing instruments. Through such creative music making, learners will con-
tinue to develop their early musical literacy skills. With the foundational knowledge of the kindergarten and grade one 
musical language previously experienced, demonstrated, and identified, there is an expectation that grade two learners 
will be able to build upon their formative learnings in order to discover the grade two musical elements. At this age, 
learners are becoming more independent in their music making. They have a good understanding of music room proto-
cols and procedures, their own and other’s importance within the music making process, and their foundational music 
language learnings that can be showcased as they are discovering the new grade two musical elements.
The music making environment should be structured in a welcoming, learner-centred way that empowers the creative 
spirit of the grade two learner. When instruments (pitched and/or non-pitched) are set up in advance by the teacher 
in the primary level learning space, there is more time for effective transitions and creative musical play. With such an 
engaging classroom set up, there are many opportunities for the music specialist and learners to create, sing, move and 
play while exploring the grade two musical elements.
The music specialist engages with the learners in their music making, facilitating an atmosphere of imaginative, creative 
and critical inquiry-based musical play while nurturing a foundational understanding of the grade two musical elements. 
Such learning can be experienced through the exploration of favourite themes and daily life experiences using simple 
songs and/or nursery rhymes with Orffestrations, storybooks and/or poems with sound stories, soundscapes, and/or 
sound carpets, plus musical centres, word play/chain rhythm fun, activities and games. This music making process allows 
an opportunity for exploration of self and others, thus encouraging an environment fostering social emotional awareness 
and diversity, equity and inclusion.

           CREATING & COMPOSING:
            IMPROVISING
CC 1.1
Learners will intrinsically want to create their own musical patterns during the creative music making process. 
Through a sound before symbol, learner-centred approach, the beginning stages of creativity, improvisation, 
and composition continue to be explored from earlier primary years into grade 2. This enables the learner to 
experience the connection and deepen their understanding of the previously learned kindergarten and grade 
one foundational elements with the new grade two elements of music. The creating and composing process 
inspires the grade two learner to communicate and express who they are within the music, as well as where 
and how their creative musical ideas work within their group’s music making.
Simple Ideas for Improvising:
During the creative music making process, learners may draw inspiration from their favourite themes or daily 
life experiences while moving, singing, vocalizing, dramatizing, visualizing, listening, and playing instruments. 
Some improvisation ideas are:
• adding a melody to a spoken phrase using their voice(s) and pitched instruments (set in a pentatonic scale)
• writing a simple poem about daily experiences (first snowfall) then using the creative music making process 
to bring the poem to life
• exploring the different characteristics of sound of all classroom instruments (timbre) and dynamics (forte 
and piano) to create a sound story and/or soundscape

           CREATING & COMPOSING:           DOCUMENTING
CC 1.2
Learners will explore how to document their musical creations with the music specialist. An audio or video 
recording can capture and preserve the learners’ creations in action, thus documenting their musical work. 
Grade two learners can also document their creations by using pictures, simple words, symbols, manipulatives, 
and/or rhythmic and melodic notation. When compositions are preserved and documented, learners can save 
them for future performance, reflection, and analysis.
Simple Ideas for Documenting:
It is important to allow learners ample opportunities in which to improvise and play with music. Learners may 
draw inspiration from various sources, including their daily life experiences or favourite themes. To bring grade 
two learners to another level in the creative process, the music specialist can then guide learners toward more 
formal compositions that they can notate themselves. Learners can notate their compositions using a variety of 
physical manipulatives and tools for showing invented and standard notation. 
Documenting ideas include:
• showing the shape of a created melody line of the music using string, scarves, and/or pipe cleaners
• notating a melody using bingo chips, magnets, and/or LEGO pieces on a three line staff
• notating a rhythm pattern using iconic and standard notation, popsicle sticks, and/or pipe cleaners
• using invented symbols/graphic notation to represent expressive elements for a class-created soundscape
There are times when learners will experience more complex musical ideas than they can developmentally 
express in written form. Such improvisations can be recorded using videos or audio recordings to preserve the 
musical ideas, thereby creating a composition. It is important to continue the safe and warm music making 
environment during the recording process.

Learners are expected to show their understanding of the musical elements through play using their voices, 
moving, dramatizing, visualizing, listening, and playing instruments. The grade two elements are discovered 
through the sound before symbol, learner-centred process in which learners and music specialists engage in 
purposeful musical play. Such creative engagement with the musical language enables the learners to fully 
experience and demonstrate the elements before recognizing and identifying those same elements in their 
musical pieces. Foundational  elements learned at the kindergarten and grade one levels will provide the 
necessary understanding that supports the learning of new grade two musical elements.
Simple Ideas for Exploring the Musical Elements through Movement: 
Grade two learners enjoy active music making and should move often. Some movement ideas are:
• discovering Mi-Re-Do, So-Mi-Re-Do, So-Mi-Do, and So-Mi-La-Do using the body scale and hand signs while singing
• exploring actions and dance movements to simple songs while singing
• creating and layering sounds to discover articulation, timbre, and dynamic effects with voices, bodies (including 
dramatization) and instruments
• feeling the quarter note pulse and/or half note pulse in the music
