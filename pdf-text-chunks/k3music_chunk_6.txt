to bring the poem to life
• exploring the different characteristics of sound of all classroom instruments (timbre) and dynamics (loud 
and quiet) to create a sound story and/or soundscape

           CREATING & COMPOSING:           DOCUMENTING
CC 1.2
Learners will explore how to document their musical creations with the music specialist. An audio or video 
recording can capture and preserve the learners’ creations in action, thus documenting their musical work. 
Grade one learners can also document their creations by using pictures, simple words, symbols, manipulatives, 
and/or rhythmic and melodic notation. When compositions are preserved and documented, learners can save 
them for future performance, reflection, and analysis.
Simple Ideas for Documenting:
It is important to allow learners ample opportunities in which to improvise and play with music. Learners may 
draw inspiration from various sources, including their daily life experiences or favourite themes. To bring grade 
one learners to another level in the creative process, the music specialist can then guide learners toward more 
formal compositions that they can notate themselves. Learners can notate their compositions using a variety of 
physical manipulatives and tools for showing invented and standard notation.
Documenting ideas include:
• showing the shape of a created melody line using string, scarves, and/or pipe cleaners
• notating a melody using bingo chips, magnets, and/or LEGO pieces
• notating a rhythm pattern using iconic and standard notation, popsicle sticks, and/or pipe cleaners
• using invented symbols/graphic notation to represent expressive elements for a class-created soundscape
There are times when learners will experience more complex musical ideas than they can developmentally 
express in written form. Such improvisations can be recorded using videos or audio recordings to preserve the 
musical ideas, thereby creating a composition. It is important to continue the safe and warm music making 
environment during the recording process.

  K-3 MUSIC - MUSA  
           MUSICAL ELEMENTS
ME 1
Learners are expected to show their understanding of the musical elements through play using their voices, 
moving, dramatizing, visualizing, listening, and playing instruments. The grade one elements are discovered 
through the sound before symbol, learner-centred process in which learners and music specialists engage 
in purposeful musical play. Such creative engagement with the musical language enables the learners to 
fully experience and demonstrate the elements before recognizing and identifying those same elements 
in their musical pieces. Foundational elements learned at the kindergarten level will provide the necessary 
understanding that supports the learning of new grade one musical elements.
Simple Ideas for Exploring the Musical Elements through Movement: 
Grade one learners enjoy active music making and should move often. Some movement ideas are:
• discovering So-Mi, So-Mi-La and melodic contour using the body scale and hand signs while singing 
• exploring actions and dance movements to simple songs while singing
• exploring letter and/or number shapes while singing
• creating and layering sounds to discover smooth and separated articulation, timbre, and dynamic effects 
with voices, bodies (including dramatization) and instruments
• feeling the steady beat, fast and slow tempos, and rest/space in the music
• moving to music to discover same and different phrase and form 
• using body percussion (snap/clap/pat/stamp) to explore rhythms within the musical language
Simple Ideas for Exploring the Musical Elements through Instruments: 
Grade one learners should explore musical patterns with their voices and bodies before playing on the 
instruments. Some instrument playing ideas are:
• showing their proper posture, mallet position and gentle playing 
• choosing instruments to create sounds effects for a favourite activity/song
• exploring solfege and melodic contour on pitched instruments
• exploring smooth and separated articulation plus quiet and loud dynamics
• exploring tone and timbre by recognizing the distinctive qualities of skin, metal, and wood instruments
• transferring word syllable patterns to body percussion and instruments
• playing and counting the number(s) in a song, poem and/or story
• playing non-pitched and pitched classroom instrument accompaniments to simple Orffestrations
• recognizing and identifying non-pitched and pitched percussion classroom instruments
• using instruments to play rhythms and melodies written in invented or standard notation
