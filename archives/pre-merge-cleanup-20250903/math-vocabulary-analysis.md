# Mathematics Vocabulary Analysis - Grade 1 Review

## Overview
The Mathematics vocabulary analysis reveals generally appropriate word choices but issues with quantity and learning goal complexity.

## Findings

### Unit: "Les nombres et les couleurs"

**POSITIVE ASPECTS:**
- ✅ Numbers 1-5 are perfect for Grade 1: un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq
- ✅ Basic colors are appropriate: rouge, bleu, jaune, vert
- ✅ All vocabulary is concrete and visual
- ✅ Words are high-frequency and useful for daily math

**ISSUES IDENTIFIED:**

1. **Too Many Words at Once**
   - Current: 10 vocabulary words in one unit
   - Issue: Cognitive overload for beginning learners
   - Recommendation: Split into 2 separate units

2. **Complex Learning Goals Language**
   - Problem: "Les élèves identifieront les couleurs..."
   - Grade 1 Alternative: "Les élèves trouvent les couleurs..."
   - Issue: Complex verb forms inappropriate for young learners

## Recommended Vocabulary Progression

### UNIT 1: "Compter 1-5" (3 words)
**Vocabulary:** un, deux, trois
**Focus:** Basic counting with objects
**Duration:** 2-3 weeks

### UNIT 2: "Plus de nombres" (2 words)
**Vocabulary:** quatre, cinq  
**Focus:** Extending counting to 5
**Duration:** 2 weeks

### UNIT 3: "Les couleurs de base" (3 words)
**Vocabulary:** rouge, bleu, vert
**Focus:** Color identification and sorting
**Duration:** 2 weeks

### UNIT 4: "Plus de couleurs" (2 words)
**Vocabulary:** jaune, orange
**Focus:** Extended color vocabulary
**Duration:** 1-2 weeks

## Language Simplification Needed

### Learning Goals - Before vs After

**BEFORE (too complex):**
"Les élèves compteront des objets jusqu'à 10 en français et identifieront les couleurs de base"

**AFTER (Grade 1 appropriate):**
"Les élèves comptent jusqu'à 5 et trouvent 3 couleurs"

### Lesson Activities - Language Fixes

**BEFORE:** "Activité de tri de couleurs : compter les ours colorés en groupes"
**AFTER:** "Jeu de couleurs : compter les ours rouges, bleus, verts"

**BEFORE:** "tout en pratiquant les nombres et couleurs en français"
**AFTER:** "en français"

## Grade 1 Math Vocabulary Guidelines

### APPROPRIATE FOR GRADE 1:
- **Numbers:** un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq (initially)
- **Basic operations:** plus, moins (not addition/soustraction)
- **Quantity:** beaucoup, peu, plus, moins
- **Size:** grand, petit
- **Shapes:** rond, carré (not cercle/rectangle)
- **Colors:** rouge, bleu, vert, jaune

### TOO ADVANCED FOR GRADE 1:
- **Operations:** addition, soustraction, multiplication
- **Concepts:** estimation, approximation, stratégie
- **Geometry:** géométrie, perpendiculaire, parallèle
- **Problem solving:** problème (use "question")
- **Abstract thinking:** analyser, comparer, justifier

### VERBS - Simple vs Complex

**USE (Grade 1):**
- compte (counts)
- trouve (finds)
- voit (sees)
- dit (says)
- fait (does)

**AVOID (too complex):**
- identifie (identifies)
- démontre (demonstrates)
- explique (explains)
- compare (compares)
- analyse (analyzes)

## Implementation Strategy

### Lesson Structure for Math Vocabulary:
1. **Show:** Visual number cards or colored objects
2. **Say:** Clear pronunciation with action
3. **Count:** Physical manipulation of objects
4. **Practice:** Simple games with concrete materials
5. **Use:** In context with real situations

### Differentiation:
- **Struggling learners:** 2-3 words per unit, extra practice time
- **Advanced learners:** 4-5 words, can connect to English cognates
- **All learners:** Concrete manipulatives essential

### Assessment:
- Student can count objects and say numbers
- Student can identify colors by pointing
- Student can sort objects by color and count
- Avoid written tests - use observation and play

## Recommendations Summary

1. **Split math vocabulary into smaller units** (3-5 words maximum)
2. **Simplify learning goal language** (use basic French verbs)
3. **Focus on concrete, manipulative-based learning**
4. **Delay complex math concepts** until later grades
5. **Emphasize visual and kinesthetic vocabulary practice**

This approach ensures Grade 1 students can successfully learn foundational math vocabulary without cognitive overload, setting them up for future mathematical success in French immersion.
