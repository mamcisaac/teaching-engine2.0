# 📚 SIMPLE LESSON EXAMPLES
## Grade 1 French Immersion Science - Wonder-Explore-Share Structure

**Purpose**: These sample lessons show exactly how the Simple Excellence format works in practice, providing concrete examples for Emily and substitute teachers.

---

## 🌟 THE WONDER-EXPLORE-SHARE PATTERN

**Every lesson follows this predictable, flexible structure:**
- **Wonder (5-8 minutes)**: Curiosity activation and learning goal
- **Explore (15-25 minutes)**: Hands-on investigation with safety
- **Share (5-10 minutes)**: Reflection and connection making

**Total Time**: 30-45 minutes (flexible based on engagement)

---

## 📋 LESSON EXAMPLE 1: LIVING OR NON-LIVING?
### Unit 1 (September) - Week 2, Lesson 8

**Materials Ready**: Collection of classroom objects (plant, rock, pencil, fish, apple, block)

#### **WONDER (6 minutes)**
**Emily gathers students in circle**

**Opening**: "Bonjour, scientifiques! Today we're going to be detectives. We'll discover which things in our classroom are living and which are non-living."

**Wonder Question**: "Look around our classroom. What do you notice about different things? Some things are alive, and some things are not alive. What do you think makes something alive?"

**Student Sharing**: Let 3-4 students share their ideas. Accept all responses positively.

**Learning Goal**: "Today we'll sort things into two groups: vivant (living) and non-vivant (non-living). Scientists look carefully to decide!"

**French Preview**: Show vocabulary cards: **vivant** (living), **non-vivant** (non-living), **observer** (to observe)

#### **EXPLORE (20 minutes)**

**Safety First (1 minute)**: "Scientists, remember our safety rules: gentle hands, look carefully, ask before touching anything new."

**Core Investigation (12 minutes)** - ALL students do this:
1. **Partner Assignment**: Students work in pairs
2. **Material Distribution**: Each pair gets 3 objects from the collection
3. **Observation Time**: "Look carefully at each object. Talk with your partner. Do you think it's living or non-living? Why?"
4. **Sorting Activity**: Partners sort their objects into two groups
5. **Teacher Circulation**: Emily visits each pair, asks "Pourquoi?" (Why?) and listens to reasoning

**Extension Options (8 minutes)** - Use based on engagement:

**If High Engagement**: 
- **Extension A**: "Find 2 more objects in the classroom to sort"
- **Extension B**: "Draw one living and one non-living thing in your science journal"
- **Extension C**: "Teach another pair why you sorted something the way you did"

**If Normal Engagement**:
- Choose Extension A or B
- Continue with core sorting and discussion

**If Low Engagement**:
- Focus on just 2 objects per pair
- More adult support and guidance
- Shorter investigation time (8-10 minutes total)

#### **SHARE (9 minutes)**

**Gathering**: "Time to share our discoveries! Bring your objects and sit in our circle."

**Reflection Questions**:
- "What was one thing you decided was living? How did you know?"
- "What was one thing you decided was non-living? What made you think that?"
- "Did you and your partner ever disagree? How did you decide?"

**French Celebration**: "Let's practice our science words! Everyone say 'vivant'... now 'non-vivant'!"

**Science Journal** (3 minutes): Students draw one living and one non-living thing from today's exploration. Write or copy the words "vivant" and "non-vivant."

**Preview Tomorrow**: "Tomorrow we'll go on a classroom living and non-living hunt! Start thinking about what we might discover."

**Cleanup**: Students help put materials away safely.

## 📋 LESSON EXAMPLE 2: MATERIAL TEXTURE EXPLORATION
### Unit 2 (October) - Week 3, Lesson 14

**Materials Ready**: Texture boxes with fabric samples, sandpaper, cotton, aluminum foil, tree bark

#### **WONDER (7 minutes)**

**Opening**: "Bonjour! Today we're going to explore how materials feel different when we touch them."

**Wonder Question**: "Close your eyes and touch your desk. Now touch your shirt. How do they feel different? Open your eyes and tell me!"

**Student Sharing**: Students describe feelings: "smooth," "soft," "hard," "rough"

**Learning Goal**: "Today we'll explore material textures and learn the French words for how things feel."

**French Preview**: **lisse** (smooth), **rugueux** (rough), **mou** (soft), **dur** (hard)

**Safety Reminder**: "We'll touch materials gently with our hands. No tasting! If something feels uncomfortable, let me know right away."

#### **EXPLORE (22 minutes)**

**Safety Check**: "Remember: gentle touches, no tasting, stop if anything feels uncomfortable."

**Core Investigation (15 minutes)**:
1. **Texture Stations**: 4 stations around room, 2-3 students per station
2. **Rotation System**: Spend 3 minutes at each station, rotate on signal
3. **Exploration Method**: "Touch each material. How does it feel? Talk to your station partner about what you notice."
4. **Vocabulary Practice**: Emily circulates, asks "Comment ça se sent?" (How does it feel?) and helps with French words

**Extension Options (7 minutes)**:

**High Engagement**:
- **Extension A**: "Sort the materials from smoothest to roughest"
- **Extension B**: "Find something in the classroom that feels similar"
- **Extension C**: "Predict what other materials might feel like these"

**Normal Engagement**:
- Choose one extension activity
- Allow extra time at favorite texture station

**Low Engagement**:
- Focus on just 2 texture stations
- Adult partner with students who need support
- Simplified vocabulary focus (just smooth/rough)

#### **SHARE (8 minutes)**

**Circle Gathering**: "Bring your favorite texture material to our sharing circle."

**Reflection Activities**:
- **Texture Game**: "I'll say a French word, and you touch something that feels that way. 'Lisse'... 'Rugueux'..."
- **Discovery Sharing**: "What was the most interesting texture you felt today?"
- **Connection Making**: "Where have you felt these textures before at home or outside?"

**Science Journal** (3 minutes): Draw two materials that feel very different. Try to write the French texture words.

**Preview**: "Tomorrow we'll test which materials are strongest! Think about which materials you think might be hard to break."

## 📋 LESSON EXAMPLE 3: WEATHER SAFETY CHOICES
### Unit 3 (November) - Week 2, Lesson 22

**Materials Ready**: Weather clothing items, weather pictures, window access for observation

#### **WONDER (5 minutes)**

**Opening**: "Bonjour, weather scientists! Look outside our window. What do you notice about today's weather?"

**Weather Observation**: Students look outside and describe what they see.

**Wonder Question**: "If you were going outside right now, what would you need to wear to be safe and comfortable?"

**Learning Goal**: "Today we'll learn how to choose the right clothing for different weather to stay safe."

**French Preview**: **temps** (weather), **sécurité** (safety), **vêtements** (clothing)

#### **EXPLORE (25 minutes)**

**Safety Focus**: "Being weather-safe means choosing the right clothing and staying together outside."

**Core Investigation (18 minutes)**:
1. **Weather Scenarios**: Emily shows pictures of different weather (sunny, rainy, snowy, windy)
2. **Clothing Choices**: Students work in teams of 3 to choose appropriate clothing for each weather
3. **Safety Discussion**: "What could happen if we don't dress right for this weather?"
4. **Real-Life Connections**: "When have you needed special clothes for weather?"

**Extension Options (7 minutes)**:

**High Engagement**:
- **Extension A**: "Design the perfect outfit for today's weather"
- **Extension B**: "What would you pack for a week with different weather each day?"
- **Extension C**: "Teach younger students about weather safety"

**Normal Engagement**:
- Choose Extension A or simple version of Extension B
- More discussion about real weather experiences

**Low Engagement**:
- Focus on just today's weather and appropriate choices
- Use real classroom clothing items for hands-on selection
- Adult support for decision-making

#### **SHARE (10 minutes)**

**Weather Safety Circle**: "Let's share our weather safety discoveries!"

**Reflection Activities**:
- **Safety Scenarios**: "If it's snowy outside, what do we need? If it's hot and sunny?"
- **French Practice**: Practice weather and safety words with actions
- **Personal Connections**: "Tell about a time weather clothing kept you safe."

**Science Journal** (4 minutes): Draw yourself dressed safely for today's weather. Write or copy weather words.

**Weather Prediction**: "Look outside again. Do you think tomorrow's weather will be the same or different? What makes you think that?"

**Preview**: "Tomorrow we'll observe how trees and plants respond to fall weather!"

## 📋 LESSON EXAMPLE 4: CELEBRATION LESSON
### Unit 10 (June) - Week 2, Lesson 180

**Materials Ready**: Science journals from the year, photo collection, favorite investigation materials

#### **WONDER (8 minutes)**

**Opening**: "Bonjour, amazing scientists! We've learned so much this year. Today we celebrate what we've discovered!"

**Memory Sharing**: "Look through your science journal. What discovery made you most excited?"

**Wonder Question**: "If you could teach someone else one thing you learned in science this year, what would it be?"

**Learning Goal**: "Today we'll celebrate our growth as scientists and share our favorite discoveries."

**Year Review**: Quick review of units: "We explored our school, materials, weather, energy, growth, movement, spring changes, earth materials, and now we're celebrating!"

#### **EXPLORE (20 minutes)**

**Safety Celebration**: "First, let's celebrate how good we've become at exploring safely! Show me gentle scientist hands... show me careful observation eyes!"

**Core Celebration (15 minutes)**:
1. **Discovery Stations**: 4 stations with favorite materials from different units
2. **Choice Exploration**: Students choose their favorite station and explore freely
3. **Peer Teaching**: Students teach each other discoveries from the year
4. **Photo Memories**: Look at photos from science investigations throughout the year

**Extension Options (5 minutes)**:

**High Engagement**:
- **Extension A**: "Design a new investigation using this year's materials"
- **Extension B**: "Write a letter to next year's Grade 1 scientists"
- **Extension C**: "Plan a summer science adventure"

**Normal Engagement**:
- Choose favorite discovery to share with another class
- Extra time to explore favorite materials

**Low Engagement**:
- Focus on looking through science journal and photos
- Share one favorite memory with a friend
- Adult support for positive reflection

#### **SHARE (12 minutes)**

**Celebration Circle**: "Time to celebrate our amazing year of science learning!"

**Growth Celebration**:
- **Safety Growth**: "How have we grown as safe scientists this year?"
- **French Growth**: "What French science words are you proud of learning?"
- **Discovery Pride**: "What discovery surprised you the most?"
- **Confidence Celebration**: "What can you do now as a scientist that you couldn't do in September?"

**Summer Scientists**: "You are all scientists now! This summer, keep wondering, keep exploring safely, and keep discovering amazing things!"

**Final Science Journal Entry**: Draw or write about being a Grade 1 scientist. What are you proud of?

**Special Recognition**: Each student receives recognition for their unique contribution to the class's scientific learning community.

## 🎯 SUBSTITUTE TEACHER QUICK REFERENCE

### **For ANY Wonder-Explore-Share Lesson:**

#### **If Things Go Perfectly:**
- Follow lesson plan as written
- Use extension activities for excited learners
- Take photos of great discoveries
- Let investigations run a bit longer if learning is happening

#### **If Students Are Low Energy:**
- Shorten Wonder time to 4-5 minutes
- Focus only on core investigation
- Provide more adult support and guidance
- Use calm, encouraging voice
- Allow extra time for cleanup and transition

#### **If Materials Are Missing:**
- Check substitute emergency box (red bin)
- Use simplified version with available materials
- Focus on observation and discussion instead of hands-on
- Window observations work for any science topic

#### **If Time Is Short:**
- Skip extension activities
- Do quick sharing (5 minutes)
- Save science journal for next lesson
- Priority: safety first, core learning second

#### **Emergency Backup Lesson (Any Unit):**
1. **Wonder**: "What do scientists notice about the world around them?"
2. **Explore**: Observe something in the classroom (plants, materials, weather outside)
3. **Share**: Draw observations in science journal
4. **Always emphasize**: gentle observation, safety, and French vocabulary when possible

### **Key Success Reminders:**
- **Safety first** - always
- **Wonder-Explore-Share** - predictable structure students know
- **Flexible timing** - adapt to student needs
- **Celebrate attempts** - especially French vocabulary tries
- **Document with photos** - simple evidence collection
- **Stay positive** - students feed off adult energy

**Remember**: These students are used to this routine. They will help you succeed! When in doubt, ask them "What do scientists do?" and they'll guide you through their learning structure.
