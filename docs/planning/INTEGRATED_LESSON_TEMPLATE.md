## 📚 Sample Integrated Lessons

### Example 1: Math-Science-French Integration
**Primary Subject**: Mathematics - Measurement  
**Date**: October Week 2  
**Unit**: Les nombres tout autour de nous

**Cross-Curricular Connections**:
| Connected Subject | Shared Element | How It Enhances Learning |
|------------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Science | Vocabulary: mesurer, comparer, observer | Students use measurement in authentic context |
| Science | Skill: Data collection | Practice recording measurements systematically |
| French | Resource: French number cards | Reinforce number vocabulary while measuring |
| Arts | Assessment: Visual representation | Show understanding through graphs and drawings |

**Minds On**:
- Sing French counting song (review 1-20)
- Mystery bag: Students feel and describe objects using French adjectives
- Connect to yesterday's science observation of leaves

**Action**:
- Measure fall leaves collected in science using non-standard units
- Record data in science journals with French labels
- Create patterns with leaves (Math) while practicing couleur vocabulary (French)
- Compare findings using mathematical language in French

**Consolidation**:
- Gallery walk sharing measurements in French
- Add best work to Math portfolio AND Science journal
- Preview: Tomorrow we'll use these measurements to create leaf art

### Example 2: French-Social Studies-Arts Integration
**Primary Subject**: Français langue première - Family Vocabulary  
**Date**: November Week 1  
**Unit**: Notre famille

**Cross-Curricular Connections**:
| Connected Subject | Shared Element | How It Enhances Learning |
|------------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Social Studies | Vocabulary: famille, maison, communauté | Real-world application of family terms |
| Arts | Skill: Portrait drawing | Visual representation supports vocabulary |
| FPS | Resource: Family photos | Personal connection increases engagement |
| Math | Assessment: Family tree counting | Practice numbers while learning relationships |

**Minds On**:
- Share family photos (brought for Social Studies)
- Count family members in French
- Introduce vocabulary through personal connections

**Action**:
- Create family portraits (Arts) with French labels
- Write simple sentences about families
- Partner sharing using question frames
- Connection to diverse families (Social Studies)

**Consolidation**:
- Family gallery with French descriptions
- Reflection: "Ma famille est spéciale parce que..."
- Take home: Family interview questions for Social Studies

### Example 3: PE-Science-Music Integration
**Primary Subject**: Physical Education - Movement and Force  
**Date**: January Week 3  
**Unit**: Fundamental Movement Skills

**Cross-Curricular Connections**:
| Connected Subject | Shared Element | How It Enhances Learning |
|------------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Science | Vocabulary: pousser, tirer, force, mouvement | Physical experience of science concepts |
| Music | Skill: Rhythm and tempo | Movement patterns reinforce musical concepts |
| French | Resource: Direction cards in French | Practice positional language authentically |
| Math | Assessment: Movement patterns | Demonstrate understanding of patterns kinesthetically |

**Minds On**:
- Simon Says with French movement commands
- Review push/pull from science
- Clap rhythm patterns from music class

**Action**:
- Movement stations exploring force
- Station 1: Pushing activities (science connection)
- Station 2: Rhythmic movements (music connection)
- Station 3: Pattern movements (math connection)
- All stations use French instructions

**Consolidation**:
- Demonstrate favorite movement with French description
- Connect to tomorrow's science lesson on force
- Cool down with French counting stretches

## 💡 Integration Tips

### Quick Wins (5-minute integrations):
1. **Transition Songs**: Use French songs that reinforce current Math or Science concepts
2. **Line-up Activities**: Practice patterns (Math) with movements (PE) using French
3. **Brain Breaks**: Simon Says with vocabulary from any subject area
4. **Material Distribution**: Count materials in French, sort by attributes
5. **Attention Getters**: Use rhythm patterns from Music class

### Medium Integration (15-minute additions):
1. **Shared Reading**: Choose books that support current Science or Social Studies
2. **Math Talks**: Use contexts from current Science investigations
3. **Art Responses**: Quick sketches to demonstrate understanding in any subject
4. **Movement Demos**: Act out concepts from Science, Math, or stories
5. **French Reflections**: End any lesson with French sentence frames

### Deep Integration (Full lesson connections):
1. **Project Launch**: Introduce project connecting 3+ subjects
2. **Investigation Stations**: Each station represents different subject lens
3. **Problem-Based Learning**: Real-world problems requiring multiple subjects
4. **Performance Tasks**: Demonstrate learning through multiple modalities
5. **Celebration of Learning**: Showcase cross-curricular connections

## 📊 Tracking Integration Success

### Student Evidence of Cross-Curricular Learning:
- [ ] Uses vocabulary from one subject in another context
- [ ] Makes connections independently ("This is like when we...")
- [ ] Transfers strategies between subjects
- [ ] Chooses to represent learning in multiple ways
- [ ] Explains learning using examples from various subjects

### Teacher Reflection Questions:
1. Which connections felt most natural?
2. What vocabulary transferred well?
3. Which resources served multiple purposes?
4. How did integration support struggling learners?
5. What connections did students discover?

### Documentation:
- Photography of integrated activities
- Anecdotal notes on transfer
- Student work samples showing connections
- Video of students explaining cross-curricular links
- Portfolio pieces demonstrating integrated learning

## 🎯 Remember: Integration Goals

The purpose of cross-curricular integration is to:
- **Deepen** understanding, not dilute it
- **Reinforce** learning, not rush through it
- **Connect** concepts, not force artificial links
- **Support** all learners through multiple entry points
- **Celebrate** the interconnected nature of learning
