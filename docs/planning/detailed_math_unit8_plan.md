# Mathematics Unit 8: Problem Solving Adventures
## Aventures de résolution de problèmes (Problem Solving Adventures)

### 📅 Unit Overview
- **Duration**: April 20 - June 25, 2026 (10 weeks, 47 instructional hours)
- **Grade**: 1 French Immersion
- **Subject**: Mathématiques
- **Unit Focus**: Multi-step problem solving, real-world applications, mathematical reasoning, Grade 2 readiness

### 🎯 Unit Purpose
This is the **culminating application unit** that integrates all learning from Units 1-7:
- Applies all mathematical skills in complex, multi-step problems
- Develops advanced problem-solving strategies and reasoning
- Connects mathematics to real-world situations and community needs
- Prepares students for Grade 2 mathematical thinking and independence
- Celebrates mathematical problem-solving as adventure and discovery

---

## 📚 PROBLEM-SOLVING ADVENTURE PROGRESSION

### Weeks 1-2 (Apr 20 - May 1): PROBLEM-SOLVING FOUNDATIONS
**Focus**: Establishing problem-solving strategies and mindset

#### Problem-Solving Strategy Development:
- **"Les Détectives Mathématiques" (Math Detectives)**:
  - Understanding what makes a problem solvable
  - Identifying known information and questions
  - Choosing appropriate tools and strategies
  - Checking and verifying solutions

- **Multi-Step Problem Introduction**:
  - Breaking complex problems into manageable steps
  - Using all mathematical skills learned (counting, addition, subtraction, measurement, patterns)
  - Connecting different mathematical concepts within problems
  - Persisting through challenging mathematical thinking

#### Real-World Problem Contexts:
- **Classroom Problems**: Organizing supplies, planning activities
- **School Community**: Helping kindergarten, planning events  
- **Family Situations**: Shopping, cooking, travel planning
- **Community Needs**: Environmental projects, helping neighbors

### Weeks 3-4 (May 4-15): MATHEMATICAL INVESTIGATIONS
**Focus**: Deep exploration through extended problem-solving projects

#### Investigation Projects:
- **"Le Jardin Mathématique" (The Mathematical Garden)**:
  - Planning school garden using measurement, geometry, counting
  - Calculating seeds needed, space requirements, growth timelines
  - Tracking plant growth with mathematical observations
  - Solving problems that arise during garden development

- **"La Fête de Fin d'Année" (End-of-Year Celebration Planning)**:
  - Calculating materials needed for class party
  - Organizing seating arrangements using patterns and spatial reasoning
  - Managing time and scheduling using measurement concepts
  - Budgeting resources using addition and subtraction

#### Mathematical Communication:
- **Problem-Solving Journals**: Daily recording of mathematical thinking
- **Strategy Discussions**: Sharing different approaches to same problems
- **Mathematical Argumentation**: Explaining why solutions work
- **Peer Collaboration**: Working together on complex investigations

### Weeks 5-6 (May 18-29): COMMUNITY PROBLEM SOLVING
**Focus**: Applying mathematics to help school and community

#### Community Service Mathematics:
- **"Aidons Notre École" (Helping Our School)**:
  - Surveying school needs and calculating solutions
  - Organizing school supplies using mathematical systems
  - Planning improvements using measurement and design thinking
  - Creating mathematical guides for younger students

- **"Mathématiques Environnementales" (Environmental Mathematics)**:
  - Calculating waste reduction in classroom and school
  - Measuring and improving recycling efforts
  - Planning sustainability projects using mathematical reasoning
  - Tracking environmental improvements over time

#### Advanced Problem Types:
- **Multi-step word problems** requiring several mathematical operations
- **Open-ended investigations** with multiple possible solutions
- **Design challenges** requiring mathematical planning and precision
- **Research projects** involving data collection and mathematical analysis

### Weeks 7-8 (Jun 1-12): MATHEMATICAL DESIGN CHALLENGES
**Focus**: Creating and solving original mathematical problems

#### Design Thinking Projects:
- **"Créateurs de Problèmes" (Problem Creators)**:
  - Students design mathematical problems for others to solve
  - Creating problems that use all mathematical skills learned
  - Testing problems with classmates and revising for clarity
  - Building problem-solving resources for future Grade 1 students

- **"L'Exposition Mathématique" (Mathematical Exhibition)**:
  - Designing mathematical exhibits for families and community
  - Creating interactive problem-solving stations
  - Developing mathematical games and activities
  - Preparing to teach mathematical concepts to visitors

#### Mathematical Innovation:
- **Invention Projects**: Creating new games or tools using mathematical concepts
- **Improvement Projects**: Using mathematics to make classroom or school better
- **Teaching Projects**: Developing ways to help others learn mathematics
- **Celebration Projects**: Planning mathematical celebration events

### Weeks 9-10 (Jun 15-25): GRADE 2 TRANSITION PREPARATION
**Focus**: Consolidating learning and preparing for next grade

#### Transition Activities:
- **"Prêt pour la 2e Année" (Ready for Grade 2)**:
  - Reviewing all mathematical skills with increased complexity
  - Solving problems that bridge Grade 1 and Grade 2 expectations
  - Building confidence for continued mathematical learning
  - Setting goals for future mathematical adventures

- **"Mentors Mathématiques" (Mathematical Mentors)**:
  - Teaching current kindergarten students basic mathematical concepts
  - Sharing problem-solving strategies with incoming Grade 1 students
  - Creating mathematical welcome activities for next year's class
  - Reflecting on growth as mathematical problem solvers

## 📊 PROBLEM-SOLVING ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

### Problem-Solving Rubric:

#### Mathematical Understanding (25%):
**Exemplary (4)**:
- Demonstrates deep understanding of all mathematical concepts involved
- Makes sophisticated connections between different mathematical ideas
- Applies mathematical knowledge flexibly and creatively
- Shows evidence of mathematical reasoning beyond grade level

**Proficient (3)**:
- Shows solid understanding of mathematical concepts used
- Makes appropriate connections between mathematical ideas
- Applies mathematical knowledge accurately and appropriately
- Demonstrates grade-level mathematical reasoning

**Developing (2)**:
- Shows emerging understanding of some mathematical concepts
- Makes basic connections between simple mathematical ideas
- Applies mathematical knowledge with some accuracy
- Shows beginning mathematical reasoning skills

**Beginning (1)**:
- Shows limited understanding of mathematical concepts
- Makes few connections between mathematical ideas
- Applies mathematical knowledge inconsistently
- Shows very basic mathematical reasoning

#### Problem-Solving Process (25%):
- **Strategy Selection**: Choosing appropriate strategies for different problems
- **Implementation**: Following through on chosen strategies systematically
- **Persistence**: Continuing to work when problems become challenging
- **Adaptation**: Changing approaches when strategies aren't working

#### Mathematical Communication (25%):
- **Clarity**: Explaining mathematical thinking clearly in French and/or English
- **Vocabulary**: Using appropriate mathematical vocabulary
- **Justification**: Supporting solutions with mathematical reasoning
- **Questioning**: Asking thoughtful questions about mathematical situations

#### Real-World Application (25%):
- **Context Connection**: Relating problems to real-world situations
- **Practical Solutions**: Developing solutions that work in real contexts
- **Community Impact**: Considering how mathematical solutions help others
- **Transfer**: Applying problem-solving skills to new situations

### Assessment Methods:
- **Problem-Solving Portfolios**: Collection of completed investigations and reflections
- **Video Documentation**: Recording students explaining their problem-solving process
- **Peer Assessment**: Students evaluating each other's problem-solving approaches
- **Self-Reflection**: Students assessing their own growth as problem solvers
- **Community Feedback**: Real-world validation of problem solutions

## 🎯 DIFFERENTIATION FOR PROBLEM-SOLVING ADVENTURES

### Support Level (Emerging Problem Solvers):
- **Simplified Problems**: Multi-step problems broken into clear, guided steps
- **Visual Supports**: Picture-based problems and graphic organizers
- **Partner Support**: Collaborative problem-solving with stronger peers
- **Extended Time**: Additional time for processing and working through problems
- **Concrete Materials**: Hands-on manipulatives for all problem-solving activities
- **Guided Reflection**: Teacher support for thinking about problem-solving process

### Standard Level (Developing Problem Solvers):
- **Varied Problem Types**: Mix of routine and non-routine problems
- **Strategy Choice**: Learning multiple approaches and choosing best one
- **Independent Work**: Solo problem-solving with available support
- **Mathematical Discourse**: Explaining thinking and reasoning in French
- **Real-World Connections**: Problems connected to students' lives and interests
- **Growth Tracking**: Monitoring improvement in problem-solving abilities

### Extension Level (Advanced Problem Solvers):
- **Complex Investigations**: Open-ended problems with multiple solution paths
- **Leadership Roles**: Helping facilitate problem-solving for other students
- **Problem Creation**: Designing challenging problems for classmates
- **Mathematical Research**: Investigating questions that go beyond curriculum
- **Mentoring**: Teaching problem-solving strategies to younger students
- **Innovation Projects**: Using mathematics to create new solutions or improvements
