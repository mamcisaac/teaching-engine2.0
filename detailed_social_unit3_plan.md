# Unit Plan: Mon histoire dans le temps
## Grade 1 Social Studies | November 18, 2025 - January 17, 2026 | 30 Hours

### OVERVIEW
**Unit Title (French):** Mon histoire dans le temps  
**Unit Title (English):** My History Through Time  
**Duration:** 9 weeks (30 hours total, including winter break)  
**Primary Focus:** Personal history, family heritage, and time concepts with strong PEI historical connections

### CURRICULUM EXPECTATIONS ADDRESSED
- **1LT.2**: Organiser les événements marquants de sa vie dans le temps
- **1ICC.1**: Décrire l'unicité des personnes et la diversité des langues et des modes de vie de sa famille et des familles de la classe (indicator: Présenter son histoire personnelle)
- **1LT.3**: Organiser différents activités et événements dans le temps

### BIG IDEAS
- Chaque personne a une histoire unique et précieuse
- Le temps nous aide à organiser et comprendre nos expériences
- Les familles ont des histoires qui se connectent à l'histoire de l'Île-du-Prince-Édouard
- Nous grandissons et changeons, mais nous gardons nos souvenirs importants
- L'histoire nous aide à comprendre le présent et planifier l'avenir

## DETAILED PERSONAL TIMELINE DEVELOPMENT ACTIVITIES

### Week 1 (November 18-22): Ma naissance et mes premières années

#### **Personal Birth Story Documentation**
**Activity Duration:** 4 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Materials:**
- Baby photo collection requests sent home
- "Mon histoire commence" booklet templates
- Timeline strips for wall display
- Seasonal birth cards (spring, summer, fall, winter babies)
- Growth measurement materials

**Process:**
1. **Birth Story Sharing:** Students share when and where they were born
2. **First Year Timeline:** Major milestones (first smile, first tooth, first steps)
3. **Hospital/Home Connections:** Where PEI babies are born (QEH, Prince County, Kings County)
4. **Seasonal Birth Exploration:** Understanding seasons when classmates were born
5. **Growth Documentation:** How we've grown since birth

**PEI Connections:**
- PEI hospitals and healthcare history
- Traditional midwifery on the Island
- Seasonal celebrations around birth (harvest babies, winter babies, spring babies)
- Island families and multi-generational presence
- Immigration stories: families who came to PEI before student birth

#### **Early Childhood Memory Collection**
**Activity Duration:** 3 sessions (45 minutes each)

**Memory Categories to Explore:**
- **First Words:** Language development (French, English, other languages)
- **First Foods:** Traditional family foods, Island specialties
- **First Friends:** Early social connections
- **First Adventures:** Trips to beaches, parks, family visits
- **First Fears and Comforts:** Understanding emotional development

**Family Interview Project:**
- Students interview parents/grandparents about early years
- Recording special moments and family stories
- Understanding how families celebrated milestones
- Learning about family traditions around early childhood

**PEI Early Childhood Connections:**
- Traditional Island lullabies and songs
- Local daycares and early childhood programs
- Beach first experiences and water safety
- First experiences with Island weather and seasons

## TIME CONCEPTS EXPLORATION ACTIVITIES

### Week 3 (December 2-6): Comprendre le temps

#### **Past, Present, Future Understanding**
**Activity Duration:** 5 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Time Concept Development:**
1. **Past Exploration:** "What happened before?" activities
2. **Present Awareness:** "What is happening now?" daily observations
3. **Future Planning:** "What will happen next?" goal setting
4. **Time Vocabulary:** hier, aujourd'hui, demain, avant, maintenant, après
5. **Time Sequencing:** Ordering events chronologically

**Interactive Time Activities:**
- **Time Machine Imagination:** Traveling to past family events
- **Present Moment Photography:** Documenting current experiences
- **Future Dreams Board:** Visualizing goals and aspirations
- **Story Sequencing:** Ordering events in familiar stories
- **Daily Schedule Mapping:** Understanding routine time patterns

**PEI Time Connections:**
- **Seasonal Time:** How Islanders mark time through seasons
- **Agricultural Time:** Planting, growing, harvest cycles
- **Cultural Time:** Traditional celebrations throughout the year
- **Historical Time:** Before Confederation Bridge, before cars, before electricity

#### **Calendar and Season Awareness Development**
**Activity Duration:** 4 sessions (45 minutes each)

**Calendar Skills Building:**
1. **Monthly Exploration:** Understanding 12 months and their characteristics
2. **Weekly Patterns:** Days of the week and weekend/weekday differences
3. **Seasonal Changes:** How seasons affect Island life
4. **Holiday Mapping:** When special celebrations occur throughout year
5. **Personal Date Recognition:** Birthdays, family anniversaries, special dates

**PEI Seasonal Calendar:**
- **Spring:** Maple syrup season, Easter, May 24 weekend opening
- **Summer:** Canada Day, Potato Blossom Festival, tourist season, beaches
- **Fall:** Harvest time, school beginning, Fall Flavours festival
- **Winter:** Christmas lights, ice fishing, winter sports, cozy indoor time

**Historical Calendar Connections:**
- How Mi'kmaq people traditionally marked seasons
- Acadian seasonal celebrations and traditions
- When different immigrant groups arrived on PEI
- Historical events that happened in different seasons

## FAMILY HISTORY INTEGRATION ACTIVITIES

### Week 4 (December 9-13): L'histoire de ma famille

#### **Grandparent and Elder Interview Project**
**Activity Duration:** 5 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Interview Preparation:**
1. **Question Development:** What to ask grandparents and elders
2. **Recording Methods:** Notes, drawings, audio recordings (with permission)
3. **Respectful Communication:** How to ask about family history
4. **Cultural Sensitivity:** Appropriate questions for different backgrounds
5. **Follow-up Skills:** How to ask for more details

**Interview Focus Areas:**
- **Childhood on PEI:** What was it like growing up on the Island?
- **Family Traditions:** Special celebrations and customs
- **Historical Events:** Changes they've seen on PEI
- **Work and Community:** How they contributed to Island life
- **Immigration Stories:** Journey to PEI (if applicable)

**Elder Wisdom Integration:**
- **Mi'kmaq Elders:** Traditional knowledge and Island history
- **Acadian Elders:** Cultural preservation and community building
- **Immigrant Elders:** Stories of building new lives on PEI
- **Long-time Islander Families:** Multi-generational Island connections

**Activity Duration:** 4 sessions (45 minutes each)

**Tradition Categories:**
1. **Food Traditions:** Special family recipes and meal customs
2. **Celebration Traditions:** How holidays and special events are celebrated

4. **Activity Traditions:** Games, crafts, and activities done together
5. **Values Traditions:** Important beliefs and principles shared in family

**PEI Family Tradition Examples:**
- **Kitchen Party Traditions:** Music and storytelling gatherings
- **Harvest Traditions:** Potato digging, garden preservation
- **Seasonal Traditions:** Ice fishing, beach bonfires, sleigh riding
- **Community Traditions:** Volunteer work, church involvement, local festivals

#### **Cultural Heritage Timelines**
**Activity Duration:** 3 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Heritage Timeline Creation:**
- **Family Origin Stories:** Where families came from before PEI
- **Immigration Journeys:** When and how families arrived
- **Settlement Patterns:** Where on PEI families first lived
- **Cultural Preservation:** How traditions were maintained and adapted
- **Community Contributions:** How families helped build PEI communities

**Diverse Heritage Representation:**
- **Mi'kmaq Heritage:** Traditional territory and ongoing presence
- **Acadian Heritage:** Deportation, return, and community rebuilding
- **British Heritage:** Loyalist and immigrant settlement patterns
- **Recent Immigration:** New families building Island connections

**Activity Duration:** 4 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Immigration Story Documentation:**
1. **Journey Maps:** Showing routes families took to reach PEI

#### **Family Artifact Exploration**
**Activity Duration:** 3 sessions (45 minutes each)

**Artifact Categories:**
- **Photographs:** Old family pictures and their stories
- **Documents:** Immigration papers, marriage certificates, school reports
- **Objects:** Tools, jewelry, household items with family history
- **Recipes:** Handwritten family recipes and their origins
- **Letters:** Correspondence that tells family stories

**Artifact Investigation Process:**
1. **Careful Examination:** What do we notice about this item?
2. **Story Gathering:** What stories does this artifact tell?
3. **Historical Connections:** What does this tell us about PEI history?
4. **Family Significance:** Why was this item kept and treasured?
5. **Present Connections:** How does this relate to our lives now?

## PEI HISTORICAL CONNECTIONS ACTIVITIES

### Week 6 (January 6-10): L'histoire de notre Île

#### **Island History Through Family Stories**
**Activity Duration:** 4 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Historical Period Exploration:**
1. **Pre-European Contact:** Mi'kmaq traditional life and seasonal patterns
2. **Early European Contact:** First meetings and cultural exchanges
3. **Acadian Settlement:** Building communities and agricultural development
4. **British Settlement:** Loyalists and immigrant waves
5. **Confederation Era:** PEI joining Canada and railway development
6. **Modern Era:** Confederation Bridge and contemporary Island life

**Family Connection Activities:**
- Mapping when families arrived in relation to historical periods
- Understanding how historical events affected families
- Identifying family members who witnessed historical changes
- Connecting family stories to broader Island history

**Historical Thinking Development:**
- **Continuity and Change:** What stayed the same, what changed?
- **Cause and Effect:** How did historical events affect families?
- **Historical Significance:** Why were certain events important?
- **Primary Sources:** Using family artifacts and stories as evidence

#### **Traditional vs Modern Life Comparisons**
**Activity Duration:** 3 sessions (60 minutes each)

**Comparison Categories:**
- **Transportation:** Horse and buggy vs. cars vs. Confederation Bridge
- **Communication:** Letters vs. telephones vs. internet
- **Work:** Traditional farming/fishing vs. modern occupations
- **Education:** One-room schoolhouses vs. modern schools
- **Entertainment:** Traditional music and storytelling vs. modern entertainment

**Family Experience Integration:**
- How grandparents/great-grandparents lived differently
- Technologies that changed family life on PEI
- Traditional skills that families still use today
- Modern conveniences that families appreciate

**PEI-Specific Changes:**
- **Agricultural Evolution:** From subsistence to commercial farming
- **Tourism Development:** From local attraction to major industry
- **Cultural Preservation:** Maintaining traditions in modern times
- **Environmental Changes:** Conservation efforts and environmental awareness

## REFLECTION AND NEXT STEPS

### **Unit Reflection Questions:**
1. Comment notre compréhension de l'histoire personnelle et familiale a-t-elle évolué?
2. Quelles connexions avons-nous découvertes entre nos familles et l'histoire de l'Île-du-Prince-Édouard?
3. Comment utilisons-nous maintenant les concepts de temps pour organiser nos expériences?
4. Quelles traditions familiales voulons-nous préserver et continuer?
5. Comment pouvons-nous continuer à apprendre sur notre héritage et notre histoire?

### **Historical Thinking Development:**
This unit establishes foundational historical thinking skills that will support all future social studies learning:
- **Chronological thinking and timeline construction**
- **Primary source analysis using family artifacts and stories**
- **Multiple perspective recognition through diverse family experiences**
- **Cause and effect understanding in historical context**
- **Cultural appreciation and heritage preservation awareness**

### **Family and Community Engagement Continuation:**
- **Ongoing Elder Interview Projects:** Building relationships with community seniors
- **Heritage Preservation Activities:** Digitizing family photos and documents
- **Cultural Celebration Participation:** Active involvement in PEI multicultural events
- **Historical Site Visits:** Family trips to relevant historical locations
- **Community History Contributions:** Sharing family stories with local historical societies

### **Transition to Future Learning:**
The personal and family history foundation established in this unit prepares students for:
- **Grade 2 Community History Studies:** Understanding local community development
- **Canadian History Connections:** Placing family stories in broader Canadian context
- **Global Citizenship Understanding:** Connecting family immigration stories to world events
- **Advanced Historical Thinking:** Building sophisticated analysis and interpretation skills
- **Cultural Identity Development:** Continued exploration of multicultural Canadian identity

This comprehensive approach ensures that personal history becomes the foundation for understanding broader historical concepts, while maintaining strong connections to PEI heritage and fostering appreciation for the diverse cultural fabric of Island communities.

### **Long-Term Impact Goals:**
- **Heritage Appreciation:** Lifelong commitment to understanding and preserving family and cultural heritage
- **Historical Empathy:** Ability to understand and appreciate different historical perspectives and experiences
- **Community Connection:** Strong sense of belonging to PEI and commitment to Island community
- **Critical Thinking:** Sophisticated ability to analyze historical information and make informed judgments
- **Cultural Bridge-Building:** Skills for connecting across cultural differences and building inclusive communities

Through this deep exploration of personal history and time, students develop both the knowledge and skills necessary for engaged citizenship and lifelong historical thinking, while maintaining strong connections to their Island home and diverse cultural heritage.
